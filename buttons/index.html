01 August · Options considered

The directions the team explored.

  • Each designer explored their own proposals on real app screens.
  • Reviewed together in DS Office Hours on 14 and 21 August.
01 21 August · DS Office Hours

The team voted unanimously to add a black neutral.

Black was seen as the most flexible option.

02 26 August · What we agreed

Hierarchy is about function first, colour second.

Nielsen Norman Group
For us
Primary
Most visual emphasis, usually solid and filled. For important or common actions: save, check out.
The happy path. One per view.
Secondary
Medium emphasis, usually an outline. For less important actions: cancel, reply.
Alternatives that don't guide the user.
Tertiary
Minimal emphasis. For optional or supplementary actions: view more.
Even less prominent.

Source: Nielsen Norman Group, Button States: Communicate Interaction

Open points: black draws a lot of attention, so it's an exception (e.g. a standalone action), not a default. Next step: map real screens with competing buttons before deciding.

03 September · First mapping: black as secondary
04 24 September · Constraints we found

A large black secondary doesn't work.

  • Too many large black buttons across the app.
  • Black competes with the primary: e.g. the two can't sit together in a modal.
  • The rules and hierarchy of the system have to hold.

So the next question was whether black could work as the tertiary.

05 Second mapping: black as tertiary
06 Constraints we found

Black as tertiary turns the hierarchy upside down.

Visual weight

Black carries far more weight than the light purple secondary, so the "least prominent" button would stand out the most.

Exceptions

Keeping it only for small sizes would need so many exceptions that the redesign loses its point.

07 Where we're going

A neutral outline tertiary.

Across 17 benchmarked systems, hierarchy steps down in weight: a filled primary, a lighter secondary, and a tertiary with the least visual weight. Our set follows the same ladder: it's the standard way to build buttons, and the one that reinvents the wheel the least.

Keeps the order

Lighter than the secondary, so primary → secondary → tertiary reads correctly.

Neutral

No brand colour on the quietest action. Black stays available as an exception.

No new tokens

Built on the Outline token group that already exists in ADS Foundations.

09 Which outline

Five outlines tested. Grey keeps the ladder intact.

Button Label

Black outline

  • Crisp and neutral, strong contrast (17.5:1)
  • Heavy: brings back the weight of black we just ruled out
  • Competes with the light purple secondary
Proposed
Button Label

Grey outline

  • Lightest weight, so it sits clearly below the secondary
  • Neutral, and the label stays fully legible (17.9:1)
  • Uses the existing Outline token
  • Border is 2.7:1, under the 3:1 guideline for UI edges
Button Label

Dark purple outline

  • On brand
  • Three purple buttons in a row: primary, secondary, tertiary
  • Reads like another secondary, so the hierarchy blurs
Button Label

Mid purple outline

  • On brand, softer than the dark purple
  • Label stays legible (11.8:1)
  • Still three purple buttons in a row
  • Border is 2.7:1, same as the grey
Button Label

Light purple outline

  • On brand and soft
  • Border nearly invisible (1.35:1)
  • Same colour as the secondary fill, so it looks like a disabled secondary
10 Two options

Two ladders to decide between.

Option A · the typical ladder
Primary Secondary Tertiary
  • Outline secondary is the norm: 13/17 benchmarked systems
  • The soft fill tertiary looks heavier than the outline secondary
Option B · closest to what we agreed
Primary Secondary Tertiary
  • Keeps today's secondary: only the tertiary changes
  • Each tier is lighter than the one above it
  • An outline tertiary is rare (1/17 benchmarked systems)

12 Challenges to the outline

What we've heard

  • It feels a bit cold.
  • It looks like a wireframe.
  • It has less warmth and personality.

Buttons don't need to bring the warmth.

The problem started when we used pink and orange buttons to add warmth, and over time we've seen how much that didn't work.

Buttons will be strong and functional. The warmth comes from colour, illustrations, background colours, photography and images, not from the buttons.

On "wireframe": our users don't know what a wireframe is, so we're not worried they'll see the product as one.
